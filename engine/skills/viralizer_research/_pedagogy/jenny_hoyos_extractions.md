# Jenny Hoyos — extractions

Source: long-form Creator Science interview. The bits below are the load-
bearing craft principles, lifted with Jenny's own words where it matters.
Search "Jenny Hoyos Creator Science" on YouTube for the full transcript.

## First frame is its own hook

> "A good short is one that has a strong hook — the way I like to explain it
> is if it could be used as a title in thumbnail on a long form and still
> get clicks, then it'll work for a short. The hook needs to be very visual
> — you need to understand it without even listening to it."

Operational test: mute the first second. Can the IVP tell what it's about?

## The mandatory second beat: foreshadow

Every Jenny video opens with the same two-line structure:

1. **Hook** — names the topic + visual.
2. **Foreshadow** — names the destination (what'll be at the end).

> "I always have spoken time to foreshadowing it. Every video. Hook +
> foreshadow + end. It just needs to have some sort of expectation."

Example: *"I'm going to the beach AND I'm going to surprise someone with
$100 at the end of the video."* The viewer now has a reason to keep
watching past the hook's grip.

Combined hook + foreshadow target: ≤3 seconds, ≤2 lines.

## Set expectations + follow through, then twist

Tell them what the payoff will be. Deliver it. Then add a small twist.

> "I gave my mom a $5 Mother's Day gift. The hook was 'my mom's never had a
> Mother's Day gift, I'm going to change that.' Expectation: I'll surprise
> her with a gift. I followed through. The twist: she dropped the gift, it
> broke. She still loved it. Expectation closed, then twisted."

## 5th-grade readability

> "I have analyzed thousands of shorts… Mr Beast is one of the best shorts
> creators and his is like in first grade level. So fifth grade and under
> is about the range you want to be at."

Tool Jenny uses: **readabilityformulas.com** (free).

Watch for category words that single-handedly bump the grade level:
*business, finance, profit*. Replace with the definition rather than the
word: *"instead of saying 'profit' I just explain the definition of profit
every time I talk about it."*

## Mechanism — the structural device that pulls to the end

> "Mr Beast: 'last to leave the circle wins $500,000.' The mechanism is the
> circle is constantly closing — so the viewer is watching till the end. If
> the circle wasn't closing I don't know if anyone would watch to the end."

Jenny's go-to mechanism for talking-head: **"there's three things"** —
visible list with countable progress.

> "I think that's why my videos go viral — people have a good expectation
> of what they're going to watch and what's actually going to be at the
> end."

## Pacing break without the pacing actually breaking

> "I used to have lots of breaks because you want to give people time to
> breathe — but you don't want to be 'I'm going to be doing this and this
> and this' video over. So instead of 'let's get started' I said 'so I
> cooked illegally.' That just flows better."

Replace meta-transitions (*"let's get started", "first thing's first"*)
with a content-carrying line that buys the same beat of breath.

## But / therefore connectors

> "I went on a walk then it started raining then I went back home. Vs:
> I went on a walk BUT it started raining THEREFORE I started running back
> home. Same story. The 'but/therefore' version sounds intriguing because
> stories can't be stories without change."

Audit the script for sequential *"and then / and then / and then"* — flat.
Replace with *but / therefore* connectors to force change-driven story.

## End abruptly

Every Jenny video ends ON the moment, not after it. She trims literally
the last 1 second of YouTube renders to lift retention by ~5 points.

> "Every second counts on a short. Whatever you say you're going to do, you
> end it right after you do it."

## Audience — specific real person, not demographic

Jenny's audience-in-her-head: her 7- and 10-year-old nieces who recently
moved to America and barely understand English.

> "It's very tough because they might not be interested in the topic — but
> if I can make them interested and especially if it makes sense to them as
> non-English speakers then it's probably really good."

Reinforces vision.md IVP rule — pick a real person you know.

## Steal-then-twist hook generation

> "I started by stealing — like an artist — copying other people's hooks
> and looking at which percentage was the highest. A strategy: 'what would
> it sound like if [big YouTuber] made this video?' Then I'd make a bunch
> of hooks, pick my favorite, see the percentage, assess. Then make my own
> twist."

Process: per script, draft 5-10 hook candidates in the voice of
top-performing reference creators in the niche; pick the strongest; twist
to fit the IVP. (Maps to viralizer §14: derive the arc, don't copy the
words.)
