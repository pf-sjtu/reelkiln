# Master patterns — what makes outliers across all 7 niches

After reading ~370 of the highest-outlier-score-and-views videos in the DB, the cross-niche patterns are stunningly consistent. The topic changes; the SHAPES don't.

---

## The 6 hook archetypes that recur in every niche

Every viral hook in the sample falls into one (sometimes two) of these:

### 1. **The Skit (Wrong → Stop → Right)**
Two-actor (or shot-reverse-shot of one creator) playing both the wrong-doer and the corrector.
- Career: "Salary expectations? Um, I don't know, $45k I guess." — STOP — "Thank you so much for bringing that up. I'd love to know the approved range." (advicewitherin, 23M)
- Sales: "Hey, this is Rob. How are you? I am just FINE." — STOP — the F-word in sales (robneidlinger, 16.1M)
- Finance: "Bill is $1000. Anyone wanna throw their card down?" — friend's debit-card reveal → "I had debit cards all wrong." (nicktalksmoney, 18M)
- Boundary-parenting: "Can I hold her? Yeah, no, not today." (ab.hightower, 526k)

**Why it works:** The viewer mentally rehearses the corrected behavior. The structural beat ("STOP, do this instead") is a hard pattern-interrupt that resets attention.

**Required elements:** specific, performable wrong-version (the cringe is what hooks); a hard pivot moment; verbatim corrected dialogue.

### 2. **The Insider Confession (with money on the line)**
A practitioner of the trade reveals a behavior of their industry that the audience didn't know about.
- "I just offered a candidate $85,000 for a job that had a budget of $130,000." (tessa, 9.4M)
- "Banks make money on your patience, not your payments." (chriscobb_chosen)
- "Things I tell my kids as a criminal defense attorney." (attorneyadamashby)
- "Client made $569,131. Tax bill: $176k → $9k." (carlottathompsonfinance)
- "I am a war-readiness trained medic..." (zayzayexpress)

**Why it works:** Authority + scarcity of access ("I'm in the room you're not"). Pairs perfectly with a counter-establishment thesis.

**Required elements:** clear credentialing (job title, years, dollar receipts); a SPECIFIC dollar/case/scenario, not abstract; an undertone of "I shouldn't be telling you this."

### 3. **The Diagnostic Listicle ("3 signs that...")**
- "These are 3 signs that you might have high-functioning anxiety." (robdial, 6.1M)
- "5 things your kids should know or you're setting them up for failure." (rorydouglas, 8.2M)
- "6 things I would never do as an asset protection specialist." (mpestateplanninguk, 5.1M)
- "Top 5 write-offs for new LLC owners." (karltondennis, 2M)
- "10 corporate truths no one is talking about." (deepali, 3.5M)

**Why it works:** Self-recognition is dopamine. Each item is its own micro-payoff. The numbered structure is a built-in retention curve.

**Required elements:** odd or specific numbers (3, 5, 7, 10 — never "a few"); each item is roughly the same length; a re-hook before #1 ("stick around for the bonus mistake at the end") and another between items 3-4.

### 4. **The Counter-Establishment Reveal**
- "LinkedIn did not intend for job seekers to read this article, but you've got to." (selfmademillennial, 1.6M)
- "Banks hate this strategy, not because it's illegal, but because it's not profitable." (chriscobb_chosen)
- "Marketers are going to hate me for talking about this..." (heytony.agency, 3M)
- "Diamond rings are a marketing campaign by De Beers." (vincentchanco, 5.8M)
- "Need to have a closed-door policy with HR. Do not talk to them." (strategytips, 1.5M)

**Why it works:** Frames the creator as on-your-side against a powerful institution. Identifies a hidden enemy (HR / banks / De Beers / LinkedIn algorithm).

**Required elements:** a named institution; an opening that signals the institution doesn't want this said; a mechanism explanation showing WHY they wouldn't want it said; a payoff the viewer can act on today.

### 5. **The Transformation/Walkthrough**
- "Let's turn this 1/10 resume into a 10/10." (greglangstaff, 12.7M) — live-narrated edit, "much better" after each step
- "This is what $130,000 gets you in Texas." — room-by-room (sanchezrealty, 6.3M)
- "Open YTB.rip → paste link → press start." (joshuamaraney) — micro-tutorial
- "If I were Kaisenad's personal speaking coach, here's what I'd give him." (npcfaizan, 1.9M)

**Why it works:** Each visible step is a micro-payoff. The viewer is being SHOWN, not told. The final reveal is anchored against the opening state.

**Required elements:** a clear before-state (visible, anchored); narration of each transformation step; a "much better" or contrast cue per step; a final reveal that's clearly the finished thing.

### 6. **The Story-as-Confession**
- "So I just got kicked out of my hotel and the authorities were called in Chautauqua." (sharonkgillenwater, 8.5M, 559s)
- "When our son was little, I didn't like him." (calmparentingpodcast, 1.4M)
- "Once upon a time, I sold a big-ass house and made $137k in commission... I pissed it away." (glenndabaker, 10.6M)
- "5 years ago I was working at an Amazon warehouse 6pm to 4am." (willchangelives)
- "Both my parents and my in-laws did some pretty insane things while raising me on the Upper East Side." (onelitmama_)
- "Abandoned mansion and a pool, he jumps in..." (doctormyro, 50.6M)

**Why it works:** Story is the oldest container for retention. Specific named characters (Bitsy, Bunny, Helen, Joe) and sensory details ($400/night, "Eastern European masseuse") create vivid mental cinema. The lesson lands sideways.

**Required elements:** a named protagonist OR antagonist; sensory specifics (numbers, brand names, places); escalating beats; a coda that re-frames meaning without lecturing.

## The re-hook palette (every 3-5 seconds is mandatory)

Re-hooks are not optional — they are the difference between a 30s viral and a scroll-past. Across all niches, the reusable forms:

**Pattern-interrupts:**
- "Stop." / "Wait." / "But here's the thing."
- "Are you paying attention?" / "Did you catch that?"
- A direct cut to a different shot — visual pattern-interrupt counts as a re-hook even if the dialogue is mid-flow

**Loop-deferrers (open the loop wider):**
- "Stick around to the end because I'll also share..."
- "And the kicker is..."
- "And yes, I'm even gonna tell you the wildest thing my mother-in-law did that wasn't covered in the tabloids." (deferred-juiciest)
- "Before I give you the final pro tip, let's talk about what NOT to say." (withholding the headline)

**Mechanism-prompts (curiosity satisfaction):**
- "Why does this work?"
- "Maybe you're wondering, how does this even work? You see..."
- "Here's why."

**Self-doubt invitations (speak directly to the viewer):**
- "I know what you're thinking — '[the objection]'. And here's the answer."
- "If you're sitting there nodding your head, good. That means you're ready."

**Listicle-internal beats:**
- "Mistake number one... mistake number two..." (counter-list inside a list)
- "And number five is the one nobody talks about."

## Open loops and the rules for closing them satisfyingly

The user named this explicitly: viral AND **satisfying**. Anyone can pose curiosity. The rare skill is *resolving* it in a way that lands.

### Open-loop rules
1. **The hook poses a question.** Either explicit ("what's the F-word in sales?") or implicit ("how to answer the trickiest interview question").
2. **The middle DEEPENS the question** before answering. "The salary range I'd love to know is..." — wait, what salary? The deepening is what keeps watch-time past 30%.
3. **The final 20% delivers a CONCRETE answer with at least one ANCHORING DETAIL.** "$65-95K range, flexible depending on the comp package." Not "be confident." Confidence is abstract; "$65-95K" is a sticky payoff.

### What makes a payoff *satisfying* (not just delivered)
A satisfying payoff combines:
- **Specificity.** Numbers, named techniques ("model of picture capture"), exact dialogue scripts, branded jargon ("coffee badging"). Abstract = unsatisfying.
- **A small surprise.** Not the obvious answer. The obvious answer would be "negotiate confidently." The viral answer is "ask for THEIR range FIRST." That sideways angle is what makes the AHA fire.
- **Re-applicability.** Viewer can plug it in tomorrow. The most viral payoffs are scripts (verbatim dialogue), formulas (1g protein per lb of goal weight), or one-rule shifts (replace "BEWARE OF DOG" with "DOG ON PROPERTY").
- **Emotional release.** Either relief (finally, someone said it!), validation (you're not alone), or righteous-anger ("Helen is a coward").
- **Closed loop with a wedge for the next loop.** "If you do that, you'll never struggle with this question again. And the bonus tip nobody talks about? — that's in my next video."

### Anti-patterns that fail the satisfaction test
- "And remember to be your authentic self." — abstract, untestable, not actionable.
- "And the rest is up to you." — abdicates the satisfying close.
- Listing the answer in slot 1 of 5 — viewer doesn't need to watch slots 2-5.
- Closing with a sponsor-name in place of an emotional beat.
- Heavy summarization ("so to recap, we covered...") — drains the payoff energy.

## Storytelling craft — making it viral AND satisfying

Some viral content is mechanically tight but emotionally thin. The truly great ones are also satisfying as STORIES. Cross-niche craft notes:

### Specificity is the entire game
Every great line has a noun, number, or name in it. Vague = forgettable.

| Vague | Specific (viral) |
|---|---|
| Many people struggle with this | I've coached 800 founders and 78 wrote the same vague mission |
| You should negotiate | I'm interviewing for roles in the 65-95K range |
| Old people without health die younger | Joe is 76, former marine, former amateur boxer, current savage |
| Some hotels are bad | The Spencer Hotel and Spa in Chautauqua, $400/night, Helen, Bitsy/Bunny/Betsy, laminated porch sign |
| Save your money | If you wait 10 years to start saving $100/month, that costs you $700,000 |

### Named characters anchor narrative
Sharon's hotel rant has Helen, Bitsy, Bunny, Betsy, "the Eastern European masseuse." The dolce diet has Joe (76, marine, boxer, savage). Specific names create cinema in the viewer's head and give the story a shape memory can grab.

### Quoted dialogue is the highest-leverage move
Tessa quotes the recruiter VERBATIM. The career-skits give the EXACT wording to use. Calmparentingpodcast quotes their grown son: "Dad, it didn't feel like you were disappointed. It felt like I was a disappointment to you."

When a video has a quoted line, the line *itself* is what gets shared in screenshots and clipped on second platforms.

### The "eat your vegetables" beat — earn the lesson
Most flat content has the structure HOOK → LESSON → CTA. Viral content has HOOK → SETUP → ESCALATION → MOMENT → LESSON-IN-PASSING → CTA. The lesson is delivered in the SHADOW of the moment, not as the moment itself.

In Sharon's hotel rant, the lesson is "small businesses can't handle normal customer behavior." But it's NEVER stated. The lesson is delivered through Helen hanging up, the laminated sign, the cleaning staff getting tipped — and the viewer extracts it themselves. That's why it sticks.

### Vulnerability is a force multiplier
- "When our son was little, I didn't like him." (calmparentingpodcast)
- "I'm a little embarrassed because it's my dirty little secret." (zayzayexpress)
- "5 years ago I was working at an Amazon warehouse 6pm to 4am with $400 in my account." (willchangelives)
- "I sold a big-ass house, made $137k commission, and pissed it away." (glenndabaker)

When the creator's specific pain or shame is on the table, audience trust spikes. Trust = retention = engagement.

### Comedy / personality is the retention multiplier
Even in serious niches:
- "Bitch, I'm paying you 400 bucks a night" (sharonkgillenwater)
- "Don't be looking at my booty" (sanchezrealty during the house tour)
- "We don't be pooping. Pooping's for sissies. Farting is for men." (sarahbanksie)
- "The unhappiest people I've met all have the same traits — extremely ambitious, but lazy as fuck." (guillaumemoubeche)

Earned profanity and specific jokes telegraph "real human, not a script." The jokes don't have to land hard; they just have to be UNGENERIC.

### The "earned profanity" rule
Don't sprinkle profanity. Use it as a 1-2x emphasis tool tied to a specific emotional beat. Used too often it loses force; used once it crystallizes the line.

## CTA architecture — bake it into the dialogue

Every champion video has a verbal CTA. The bad-script anti-pattern is "follow for more" pasted at the end. Working CTA forms:

1. **Reassurance + Follow** — "You got this. Follow me for more."
2. **Gateway-to-resource** — "Comment GUIDE and I'll send you the free tutorial." (asks for engagement that EARNS something)
3. **Save-this-for-later** — "Save this and watch tomorrow morning before you open email." (specific use case)
4. **Self-identification + Comment** — "If you can relate to any of these 3, drop it below and let's talk."
5. **Share-trigger** — "Send this to somebody who [audience description]."
6. **Daily-show parasocial** — "I'll see you tomorrow." (implies cadence, builds habit)
7. **Bookmark with smiley** — "You might wanna bookmark this for future reference, just saying :)" (low-key authority)
8. **Polarizing close** — "Disagree? Let me know in the comments." (drives debate engagement)

Combine 2-3 of these. NEVER skip the verbal CTA.

## Anti-patterns shared across niches

These appear in low-performers, never in champions:

- "Hi everyone, [name] here, and today we're going to talk about..."
- "So..." as the opener (always cut)
- Long author-credentialing intros (max 1 mid-content credential drop)
- Vague morality statements ("self-care matters", "show up authentically")
- Ad-pasting CTAs (URL pill at end with no verbal callout)
- Over-summarization at the end
- Throat-clearing words: "actually", "basically", "literally" (used non-literally), "I think", "kind of", "sort of"
- AI-tells: rule of three, em-dash overuse, "It's not just X, it's Y" parallelism, neutral fact-listing, every sentence the same length

## The single most underrated craft technique

**Cuts as re-hooks.** A framing change, B-roll insert, kinetic-typography card, or shot-reverse-shot is itself a re-hook even when the dialogue is mid-flow. Most champion scripts assume a cut every 2-3 seconds even when the talking continues.

This is operationalized in our pipeline via timeline-segmentation language inside long Seedance chunks (CLAUDE.md rule 8 + seedance_prompting_guide §9-10). The script writer specifies camera moves AT word-level — "[00:03-00:07] Cut to tight close-up, slight push-in" — because the framing variety IS the retention.
